Press Release: Excessive Social Media Use Among College Students Leading to Mental Health and Social Interaction Issues

November 23, 2024, Beijing — In recent years, social media has become an integral part of university students' lives. However, a new study reveals that excessive use of social media has had a significant negative impact on their mental health and social interactions. Experts are warning that prolonged social media engagement not only affects their ability to engage in real-world social settings but also exacerbates issues such as anxiety and depression.

The Double-Edged Sword of Social Media Social media platforms like Weibo, WeChat, Instagram, and TikTok have become essential tools for colledge students' daily activities. According to recent data, over 70% of young people between the ages of 18 and 30 in China spend several hours a day on various social media platforms. While these platforms offer easy access to entertainment, social interaction, and real-time news, they also pose potential mental health risks.

Rising Mental Health Concerns According to the 2024 China Youth Social Media Usage and Mental Health Report, nearly half of the surveyed young people reported experiencing anxiety, insomnia, and low self-esteem due to excessive use of social media. This problem became more prominent after the COVID-19 pandemic, when more young people spent time working and studying from home. Many turned to social media for emotional support, yet the virtual interactions failed to satisfy deeper emotional needs, leading to feelings of loneliness and increased anxiety. Professor Li Na, a psychologist at Peking University, commented: “Social media allows young people to access information and entertainment quickly, but over-reliance on it means they are disconnected from the real world. The instant emotional feedback from likes, comments, and shares is shallow and does not fulfill the deeper emotional needs that face-to-face interactions can provide.”

How to Address This Issue? Experts suggest that young people should develop healthier social media habits. While moderate use of social media can foster social interactions and broaden one's perspective, overuse can have detrimental effects. Parents, educators, and society as a whole need to focus on guiding young people to use these platforms more consciously and responsibly. Moreover, many mental health professionals emphasize the importance of fostering real-life social skills, providing psychological support, and encouraging physical activity, all of which can mitigate the negative impact of social media addiction.
